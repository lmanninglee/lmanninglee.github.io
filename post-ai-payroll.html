Would You Trust AI With Your Paycheck?

AI & Payroll · July 2026 · Lucas

Here's a question I never expected to think about before this summer: how would you feel if a machine — not a person — calculated your paycheck?

According to a survey covered by HR Dive, a lot of Americans already have an answer. In a PayrollOrg poll of more than 25,000 workers, 34% said they're uncomfortable with AI calculating their wages. Even more striking, 45% opposed AI handling their payroll questions instead of a real person.

Why this hits home for me

I'm spending this summer as a sales intern at ADP, one of the biggest names in payroll and HR technology. AI comes up constantly — in our products, our pitches, and our clients' questions.

Here's the thing I've learned on the sales floor: payroll isn't just a transaction. It's the most personal number in someone's work life. When it's wrong, trust in the whole company takes a hit.

So workers' skepticism doesn't strike me as anti-technology. It strikes me as completely rational. People aren't rejecting AI — they're asking who's accountable when the math is wrong.

The companies that will win

The report's recommendation lines up with what I've seen in the industry: keep humans in the loop. Use AI to catch errors and speed things up, but keep a person accountable for the outcome.

Firms like OnPay make a similar case for small businesses: AI works best as a first line of support, not a replacement for judgment.

If I end up building a career in this industry, that's the version of AI I want to sell — the kind that makes payroll people better, not invisible.
