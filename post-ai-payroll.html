Would You Trust AI With Your Paycheck?

AI & Payroll · July 2026 · Lucas

Now here's something that I did not expect to think about until this summer: What if a computer, not a human, calculated your paycheck?

According to a survey covered by HR Dive, the reality is that many Americans are already able to answer this. According to a PayrollOrg survey of over 25,000 employees, 34% reported feeling uncomfortable having AI determine their paychecks. Even more notably, 45% of these individuals rejected using AI to answer their payroll questions.

Why this hits home for me

This summer I am working as an intern in sales at ADP, which is one of the most prominent companies in the field of payroll and HR technologies. The topic of artificial intelligence arises all the time.

Now this is what I have learned from the sales floor: Payroll isn't just a transaction, it is the most personal number of a person's professional life. When payroll goes wrong, it shakes up the entire organization.

So it doesn’t seem to me that the people’s skepticism about this technology is any sort of antitechnology stance. It seems to be perfectly reasonable. People aren’t refusing AI, but are demanding that there be someone responsible for the mistakes in the numbers.

The companies that will win

This particular recommendation by the report fits my experience in the industry: use AI to help a human do his job faster, but keep a person accountable for the outcome.

Firms like OnPay make an argument in favor of smaller firms on this issue too: AI is good at being an initial point of assistance, not a substitute for decision making.

In case I ever decide to make my career in this sector, I will always sell this version of AI, the one that makes payroll professionals more efficient, not invisible.
