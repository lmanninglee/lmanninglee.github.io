What Selling to Canada Taught Me About Assumptions

Cross-Border Business · July 2026 · Lucas

My internship this summer has a twist I didn't see coming. I work in ADP's Allentown, Pennsylvania office — and every single client our team serves is Canadian.

Before this summer, I would have assumed selling to Canada from the U.S. would be basically the same job. Same language (mostly), similar culture, huge shared border. How different could it be?

Ask Target how different it can be

Very, it turns out. An eMarketer analysis of brands crossing the U.S.–Canada border is full of cautionary tales. Target's Canadian expansion collapsed after supply chain and merchandising problems it couldn't overcome, and companies like Lowe's, Nordstrom, and Bed Bath & Beyond have also pulled out of Canada.

Meanwhile, Canadian brands like lululemon and Aritzia are thriving in the U.S. The border clearly isn't a wall — but it isn't nothing, either.

What it looks like from a sales desk

I see the small version of this every day. Payroll and HR run on rules, and Canada's rules are genuinely different — provincial regulations, different tax structures, different employment norms.

Even the sales tax system works differently, with GST/HST replacing the state-by-state patchwork Americans know, as TaxCloud explains in detail.

The lesson I'm taking into my career: proximity is not similarity. The best salespeople I've watched this summer never assume — they ask, they research, and they treat every market like it's new. Because it is.
