What Selling to Canada Taught Me About Assumptions

Cross-Border Business · July 2026 · Lucas

There is something surprising about my summer internship that I did not expect. I am interning at ADP's office in Allentown, Pennsylvania, but all of the clients we serve are from Canada.

Prior to my internship, I was under the impression that sales to Canada from the U.S. would be pretty much the same as domestic sales. It is a common language, similar culture, and a huge common border.

Ask Target how different it can be

Very, it turns out. An eMarketer analysis of brands crossing north into Canada is full of cautionary tales. Target's Canadian expansion collapsed after supply chain and merchandising problems it couldn't overcome, and companies like Lowe's, Nordstrom, and Bed Bath & Beyond have also pulled out of Canada.

Canadian brands, however, have been flourishing south of the border with lululemon and Aritzia leading the way. It is not a barrier by any means, but it is something.

What it looks like from a sales desk

This happens all the time with me because there are processes involved which need to be followed, and the processes in Canada are different from the processes followed in America.

The sales tax process is different because instead of the state-by-state sales tax process, the sales tax is based on GST/HST, as TaxCloud explains in detail.

So, the most important thing that I have learned from being here for the summer is that proximity does not necessarily mean similarity. The most successful salesmen are the ones who do their homework first before making an assumption.
