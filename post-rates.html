When the Fed Funds Rate Stopped Being a Homework Problem

Economics · July 2026 · Lucas

In my economics coursework at Penn State, the federal funds rate was a number in a problem set. You'd plug it into a bond pricing formula, sketch a yield curve, and move on.

Then I started my sales internship — and realized that same number quietly sits underneath almost every business conversation I'm part of.

Where rates stand right now

After cutting three times in 2025, the Fed has held the federal funds rate in a range of 3.5% to 3.75%. According to Yahoo Finance, the Fed's own "dot plot" projections put the median forecast at 3.4% by the end of 2026 — implying just one more small cut this year.

Translation: borrowing is cheaper than it was at the 2023 peak, but nobody should expect a return to the near-zero rates of 2020.

Why a sales intern cares

Small and mid-sized businesses — the exact companies I talk to — feel rates directly. Loans, lines of credit, and expansion plans all get priced off this number.

When money is expensive, businesses scrutinize every recurring cost, including the HR and payroll services I help sell. When rates ease, growth conversations open back up. You can practically hear the Fed in the tone of a discovery call.

That's the biggest thing this summer has taught me about economics: the models are real. They just show up wearing a polo shirt, asking whether now is the right time to hire employee number eleven.
