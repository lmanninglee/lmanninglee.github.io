When the Fed Funds Rate Stopped Being a Homework Problem

Economics · July 2026 · Lucas

As far as I know from my economics classes in Penn State, the federal funds rate used to be a number in some problem set. One would use it in calculations for bond prices, draw a graph of yield curves and continue with their life.

And here comes my internship in sales when I discovered that this very number lies underneath almost all of my business conversations.

Where rates stand right now

After cutting three times in 2025, the Fed has held the federal funds rate in a range of 3.5% to 3.75%. According to Yahoo Finance, he Fed's projections in their "dot plot", the median forecast for 2026 is expected to be 3.4%, which suggests one more cut in the current year.

In other words: borrowing became cheaper compared to its maximums in 2023, but no one should expect the rates of near-zero level seen in 2020 to be returned back.

Why a sales intern cares

Small and medium businesses, which is exactly who I speak with, know how interest rates work. Financing options, credit lines, and expansion efforts are all valued based on this number.

When costs are high, companies review their expenses, including the services that I market like HR and payroll. When rates fall, discussions about growth become easier again. You can hear it in the way the conversation starts when the Fed has eased its stance.

What this summer has shown me more than anything else about economics is that the economic theories are real, they just dress down in a polo shirt and ask if now is a good time to hire the eleventh employee.
